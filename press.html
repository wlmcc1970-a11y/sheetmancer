What it does

  • Reads your paper into a digital character. Photograph or upload your pages — typed, printed, PDF, or handwritten — and each Character Scan takes in up to 10 pages of one character (the main sheet plus the spell lists, skills and notes that go with it), not just the front page.
  • Any game system — and it keeps your game's own terms. It reads the character in front of it rather than assuming a ruleset, so nothing gets renamed to fit a template: Hull Integrity stays Hull Integrity, Sanity stays Sanity.
  • Make it yours. Every field is editable by hand; rename or reorder sections, add gear, log XP and level up, search the whole character, pick from seven themes, and view and edit offline after the scan.
  • Share a print-ready PDF of any character — for your table or your GM — or back it up to a file you keep.
  • Session Journal. Keep session notes and campaign history alongside the character they belong to.
  • Photo Album. A per-character gallery for maps, minis, tokens and art, synced across devices.
  • Dice Library & Calculator. A built-in toolkit of 30+ randomizers for any system — standard and unusual dice, oracles, cards and tables, a directional compass set, and an original story deck — plus a normal calculator. It never touches your character data.
  • Fallen Heroes. Archive a retired or fallen character and bring them back anytime.
  • Syncs across your devices when you're signed in, so your characters follow you.

What it doesn't do — the honest limits

  • It needs an internet connection to scan. The reading happens on a server, not on the phone. Everything else — viewing and editing your saved characters, the Dice Library, the journal — works without it.
  • Messy handwriting produces mistakes. It's a reader, not a mind. Expect to glance over a scanned character and fix a field or two, the same way you'd proofread a photocopy.
  • A dense, multi-page character takes a couple of minutes, not a couple of seconds. Ten crowded pages is real work.
  • One scan, one character. It won't digitize a whole party in a single pass.

I'd rather you knew all of this going in than ran into it mid-review.

How the scan works

Sheetmancer uses a machine-vision model to read photos of a character you already own and drop that text into editable fields. That's the only thing the technology does here: read pages you chose to photograph — the same job a document scanner or a visual-lookup app does.

It does not generate characters, art, rules, or lore. It doesn't write anything that wasn't already on your paper, and every field is yours to correct by hand afterwards. I also didn't use any generative AI for the app's own artwork, icons, store listing, or written copy — that all comes from me.

Exactly how your data is handled is spelled out in the Sheetmancer Privacy Policy, and you can delete your account and everything tied to it from inside the app whenever you want (instructions here).

About the studio

DigiRune Studios is me, Will McCullough, working alone out of Beaufort, South Carolina. I build official companion apps for independent tabletop publishers, plus standalone tools like this one for the wider tabletop community. I made Sheetmancer for a simple reason: retyping a paper character into a computer is miserable, and I wanted the paper I already had to just become the digital version. It's a standalone app, no subscription — it reads the character you already own, whatever game it came from.

Sheetmancer is a universal tool for tabletop roleplaying games and is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any game publisher. Showing a licensed game's sheet being scanned is legitimate demo use; I keep publisher logos out of thumbnails.
