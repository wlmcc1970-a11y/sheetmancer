What it does

All of your character info pages, off the page.

Reads your paper into a digital characterPhotograph or upload your pages (typed, printed, PDF, or handwritten) and each Character Scan takes in up to 10 pages of one character (the main sheet plus the spell lists, skills and notes that go with it), not just the front page.
Keeps your game's own termsAny system: it reads the character in front of it rather than assuming a ruleset, so nothing gets renamed to fit a template. Hull Integrity stays Hull Integrity, Sanity stays Sanity.
Make it yoursEvery field is editable by hand; rename or reorder sections, add gear, log XP and level up, search the whole character, pick from nine themes, and view and edit offline after the scan.
Print-ready PDFExport any character as a print-ready PDF (for your table or your GM) or back it up to a file you keep.
Session JournalKeep session notes and campaign history alongside the character they belong to.
Photo AlbumA per-character gallery for maps, minis, tokens and art, synced across devices.
Dice Library & CalculatorA built-in toolkit of 32 tools for any system: standard and unusual dice, oracles, cards and tables, a directional compass set, and an original story deck, plus a normal calculator. It never touches your character data.
Fallen HeroesArchive a retired or fallen character and bring them back anytime.
Syncs across your devicesWhen you're signed in, your characters follow you.

What it doesn't do

The honest limits.

  • It needs an internet connection to scan. The reading happens on a server, not on the phone. Everything else (viewing and editing your saved characters, the Dice Library, the journal) works without it.
  • Messy handwriting produces mistakes. It's a reader, not a mind. Expect to glance over a scanned character and fix a field or two, the same way you'd proofread a photocopy.
  • A dense, multi-page character takes a couple of minutes, not a couple of seconds. Ten crowded pages is real work.
  • One scan, one character. It won't digitize a whole party in a single pass.

I'd rather you knew all of this going in than ran into it mid-review.

How the scan works

It reads. It doesn't invent.

Sheetmancer uses a machine-vision model to read photos of a character you already own and drop that text into editable fields. That's the only thing the technology does inside the app: read pages you chose to photograph. It is the same job a document scanner or a visual-lookup app does.

It does not generate characters, art, rules, or lore. It doesn't write anything that wasn't already on your paper, and every field is yours to correct by hand afterwards. Character portraits are pictures you add yourself.

Our own artwork was made by DigiRune Studios with the help of AI image and video tools, and we label it that way in the stores.

Exactly how your data is handled is spelled out in the Sheetmancer Privacy Policy, and you can delete your account and everything tied to it from inside the app whenever you want (instructions here).

About the studio

Made by one person, on purpose.

DigiRune Studios is me, Will McCullough, working alone out of Beaufort, South Carolina. I build official companion apps for independent tabletop publishers, plus standalone tools like this one for the wider tabletop community. I made Sheetmancer for a simple reason: retyping a paper character into a computer is miserable, and I wanted the paper I already had to just become the digital version. It's a standalone app, no subscription; it reads the character you already own, whatever game it came from.

A bit about me: I'm a U.S. Marine veteran (Combat Engineer, Senior Drill Instructor), a 25-year entrepreneur, a developer and a lifelong tabletop gamer. Before DigiRune I was a broker-owner who represented 1,000+ real-estate clients, and as Governor-appointed Chairman of the State Athletic Commission I brought the first-ever UFC event to South Carolina. Precision and accountability were never optional in any of those roles, and they aren't here either.

Sheetmancer is a universal tool for tabletop roleplaying games and is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any game publisher. Showing a licensed game's sheet being scanned is legitimate demo use; I keep publisher logos out of thumbnails.
